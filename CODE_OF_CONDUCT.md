# Kuali community code of conduct

Kuali exists to make private, useful meeting transcription accessible to
everyone. A healthy project requires technical disagreement without personal
hostility and careful handling of the sensitive data that meeting software can
process.

## Our commitment

Maintainers, contributors, and community members commit to providing a
welcoming environment regardless of age, body size, disability, ethnicity, sex
characteristics, gender identity or expression, experience level, education,
socioeconomic status, nationality, appearance, race, religion, sexual identity
or orientation, or technology choices.

## Expected behavior

- Be respectful, specific, and constructive in reviews and discussions.
- Critique ideas and code rather than the person presenting them.
- Accept feedback gracefully and correct mistakes openly.
- Give credit for ideas, testing, documentation, and code.
- Respect project boundaries around privacy, consent, and participant identity.
- Remove meeting content, names, tokens, secrets, and private endpoints from
  public reports unless every affected person explicitly agreed to share them.

## Unacceptable behavior

- Harassment, threats, insults, sexualized attention, or discriminatory remarks.
- Publishing another person's private information without permission.
- Deliberately submitting meeting data, credentials, malware, or unsafe proof of
  concept code to public project surfaces.
- Sustained disruption, impersonation, attribution abuse, or bad-faith review.
- Retaliation against someone who reports a concern.

## Scope and enforcement

This code applies in the repository, issue tracker, pull requests, discussions,
project-managed community spaces, and public interactions representing Kuali.

For ordinary disagreements, ask a maintainer in the relevant thread. For a
sensitive conduct concern, use
[GitHub private reporting](https://github.com/igarrux/kuali/security/advisories/new)
and state clearly that the report concerns community conduct. Do not expose the
affected person in a public issue.

Maintainers may edit or remove content, issue a warning, temporarily restrict
participation, or permanently remove someone from project spaces. Decisions
will consider impact, context, safety, and any pattern of behavior. Maintainers
must recuse themselves from reports in which they are directly involved.
